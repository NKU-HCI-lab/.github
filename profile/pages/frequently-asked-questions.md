# Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I get compensated for my time?
Let's clarify a couple of things first.
1. Research is an additional opportunity for learning about topics in a much deeper way than you do in class. When you join a research project, your primary objective should be to gain experience.
2. When you are working on a research project, you are not working *for* your research advisor. Instead, you are working to better yourself personally and professionally. Guidance from your research advisor can help you achieve results more efficiently.

Most research work (at NKU and elsewhere) is not compensated financially. Typically, students join research projects to gain invaluable expertise they can use when searching for a job, interviewing for a position, or applying to Master's and 
Ph.D. programs. Even research advisors do not receive additional compensation and are not required to involve students in research: they work on research as an investment in students and projects.

As a result, you should not expect any compensation, especially if it is the first time you are doing research. However, you can [get course credit](#getting-course-credit) for your research work.

Your involvement in projects (i.e., tasks, expected time commitment, ...) is completely negotiable and based on your goals and availability.

### Research funding and assistantships
NKU provides students with several paid opportunities, such as UR-STEM or the ISRCA project award. These are very competitive but worth applying for. You can also apply for external Fellowships and scholarships.

In addition to funding from NKU units, paid research assistantships might be available. Feel free to ask the Head of the HCI Lab whether there is any available funding. Paid opportunities are commensurate with student experience and effort.

### Getting course credit
You can combine research with coursework by enrolling in a 0-3 credit-hour repeatable course that usually counts towards electives or experiential learning. Take advantage of this opportunity by talking to the Head of the HCI Lab.

If you exceed 16 credit hours per semester, NKU charges the course based on standard credit-hour tuition.
